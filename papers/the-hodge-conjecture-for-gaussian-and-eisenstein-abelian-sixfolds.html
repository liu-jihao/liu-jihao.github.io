The Hodge conjecture for Gaussian and Eisenstein abelian sixfolds

We prove the Hodge conjecture for Gaussian and Eisenstein abelian sixfolds, that is, abelian sixfolds whose endomorphism algebra contains the Gaussian integers or the Eisenstein integers. In particular, the Weil classes on Gaussian and Eisenstein abelian sixfolds of Weil type are algebraic for every discriminant of the Hermitian form. We also prove the Hodge conjecture for any nonsimple abelian sixfold, except for the sixfolds isogenous to products of a fourfold and a surface of one explicit type.

Update (2026-10-27 10:59:48 UTC): overlapping work. Several papers posted on Hexagon before this paper was posted here overlap with it. Theorem 1.3 for K = Q(√−3), for every discriminant, is Theorem B of B. Severn, Hexagon 2610.00387 (posted 2026-10-20); its case of discriminant −2 is also in Severn, Hexagon 2610.00246 (2026-10-14), and in A. Hippeläinen, Hexagon 2610.00371 (2026-10-20). For K = Q(i), Severn, Hexagon 2610.00477 (2026-10-22), proves Theorem 1.3 for the discriminant [−3]. Severn’s method (Galois covers with monodromy G34, Brauer induction, split eightfolds and contraction to sixfolds) is close to the one used here, and his Theorem A on all split eightfolds of Weil type for Q(√−3) contains parts (1)–(3) of Construction-Theorem 4.4. The CM case of Theorem 1.2 is also proved by P. van Hoften and J. Sempliner, Hexagon 2610.00259 (2026-10-15), which the paper cites. Not in these papers: Theorem 1.3 for K = Q(i) and the discriminants other than [−1] and [−3], and hence the Hodge conjecture for the Gaussian sixfolds; Theorem 1.2 as a single statement; Theorem 1.4 on nonsimple sixfolds, apart from the CM ones.

Details of the overlap
  • B. Severn, Weil classes on every abelian sixfold of Weil type for Q(√−3), Hexagon 2610.00387 (posted 2026-10-20; an earlier version on Zenodo, 2026-10-16). Theorem A: the Weil classes of every polarized abelian eightfold of Weil type for Q(√−3) with split discriminant are algebraic; this contains parts (1)–(3) of Construction-Theorem 4.4 here. Theorem B: the Weil classes of every polarized abelian sixfold of Weil type for Q(√−3), of any discriminant, are algebraic; this is Theorem 1.3 for K = Q(√−3). Theorem C: if moreover the Hodge group is SUH, every Hodge class on every power of the sixfold is algebraic; powers are not treated here. Its Corollary 12.1 treats, with all powers, the members with generic Hodge group of the type III families whose quaternion algebra contains Q(√−3). Its Theorem 5.1 and Corollary 4.7 (local monodromies conjugate to their inverses) correspond to Theorem 3.11 here, under a different hypothesis. The route is the same as in Sections 3–6 here: Galois covers of P1 with monodromy G34 and ten pairs of reflections, Schoen’s cycles, integral Brauer induction, and the passage from split eightfolds to sixfolds by Schoen’s product construction with a Landherr completion (compare Lemma 6.1). Severn proves that his eightfold family dominates (period rank 16 at a different maximal degeneration), whereas Corollary 5.5 here gives rank at least 15. His version of 2026-10-22 also proves the conclusion of Schoen’s Lemma 2.8 for every cyclic order, as Appendix B does here, by a different argument.
  • B. Severn, A transfer theorem for Weil classes on Prym–Tyurin factors of Galois covers, Hexagon 2610.00386 (2026-10-20; Zenodo, 2026-10-16): the general transfer theorem used in 2610.00387; its Corollary 11.1 is the case K = Q(i), discriminant [−3], conditional on a hypothesis (C).
  • B. Severn, Algebraicity of Weil classes on abelian sixfolds of Weil type for Q(√−3) with discriminant −2, Hexagon 2610.00246 (2026-10-14), and A. Hippeläinen, Algebraicity of Weil classes on non-split abelian sixfolds of Weil type over Q(√−3) with Hermitian determinant −2, Hexagon 2610.00371 (2026-10-20): the case of discriminant −2 of Theorem 1.3 for Q(√−3), by covers with monodromy G32. Hippeläinen also points out the gap in Schoen’s argument that Appendix B addresses, and avoids it.
  • B. Severn, Weil classes on abelian sixfolds of Weil type for Q(i) with discriminant −3, Hexagon 2610.00477 (2026-10-22): Theorem 1.3 for K = Q(i) and the discriminant [−3], by covers with monodromy W(E8)·μ4 branched over twelve points (sixfolds directly, without eightfolds), and the Hodge conjecture for all powers when the Hodge group is SUH.
  • P. van Hoften and J. Sempliner, The Hodge Conjecture for CM abelian varieties, Hexagon 2610.00259 (2026-10-15): the CM case of Theorems 1.2 and 1.4; cited in the paper.
  • Outside Hexagon: B. Severn, Hodge classes on powers of abelian varieties of Weil type and of type IV with exact endomorphism algebra (Zenodo, 2026-10-22), overlaps Propositions 7.1 and 7.2(2), Lemma 7.3 and Theorem 7.4(2) here (the Hodge groups in these cases, and the Hodge conjecture for all powers); K. Saller, Split Weil structures on abelian sixfolds and the Hodge conjecture (Zenodo, 2026-10-09): its Theorem C is the criterion of Proposition 7.8 here.

Only in the present paper: Theorem 1.3 for K = Q(i) and every discriminant other than [−1] (the split case, known before) and [−3] (Severn), and with it the Hodge conjecture for the Gaussian sixfolds; the Gaussian eightfold family with monodromy μ4W(E8) (Construction-Theorem 4.5), its period rank 15 (Theorem 5.7), and the transversality argument with a moving surface (Lemma 6.4, Proposition 6.5), which completes the Gaussian case without a dominant family; Theorem 1.2 as a single theorem; Theorem 1.4 and Section 8 on nonsimple sixfolds, apart from the CM ones and from the cases of Theorem 8.2 that follow from Severn’s theorems (K = Q(√−3), and K = Q(i) with discriminant [−3]); Proposition 3.7 (paired branching gives a rational Lagrangian subspace) and the contraction in Theorem 3.11, which does not use Milne’s theorem; the proof in Appendix B.
